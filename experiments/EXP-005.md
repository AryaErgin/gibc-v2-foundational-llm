# EXP-005 — Architecture Allocation Preflight

## Question

At approximately matched 21M trainable parameters, does a 24-layer, 256-wide decoder-only Transformer outperform a 10-layer, 384-wide Transformer when both train from scratch on the exact frozen EXP-004 Data Recipe v1 token stream?

## Frozen controls

Both candidates reuse the exact EXP-004 stream and dual validation tensors without copying or rematerializing them. The stream SHA-256 must be `8e727fa2a2614751a1c34d7f9ac411dfebeb379a09f584ba4f7f418d1059cea1`; the tokenizer SHA-256 must be `c5592fba176c3d2f7915a3812559a24d7a669206f4a22484b053c8a9ce08be14`. Context, initialization, optimizer, BF16/FP32 precision, seed, effective and physical batch, schedule values, 9,156-update horizon, and 300,023,808-token budget remain fixed.

EXP-005A is deep/thin: d_model 256, 24 layers, 8 heads, head_dim 32, d_ff 1024; required parameter count 20,984,064. EXP-005B is wide/shallow: d_model 384, 10 layers, 12 heads, head_dim 32, d_ff 1536; required parameter count 20,848,512. No depth-scaled initialization, residual scaling, SwiGLU, LR change, or other architectural modification is authorized.

## Preflight scope

Only equal bounded runs validate construction, finite forward/backward behavior, checkpoint/resume, the frozen sequential stream, dual validation, and runtime/memory characteristics. Short-run losses are engineering diagnostics, not architecture results. Full EXP-005A/B training and official benchmark evaluation are not authorized.

## Eventual decision rule

The primary eventual result is equal-token `combined_validation_loss = (general_validation_loss + edu_validation_loss) / 2`. If final candidate combined losses differ by under 0.02 nats, loss alone does not establish architecture superiority; efficiency and scaling evidence determine the engineering choice. This is a project decision threshold, not a significance claim.
